\title{\bfseries Shrinkage Baselines for GRPO on GSM8K\\
\large Does James--Stein shrinkage of the group mean improve GRPO?}
\author{Hanwen Yang}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
GRPO estimates each prompt's baseline from the $N$ completions sampled for
that prompt alone; with $N$ small this mean is noisy. The assignment proposes
replacing it with a James--Stein estimator that shrinks each group mean toward
the batch mean. I implemented the specification, six baselines behind one
registry, and measured them on Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct / GSM8K on a single
RTX~5080.

Three findings. \textbf{(i)} The specification's formula applied to raw $0/1$
rewards does not work: its $V=1/N$ assumes the unit per-sample reward variance
the specification says to establish first, but $0/1$ correctness rewards have
$p(1-p)\le\tfrac14$ and the pseudocode takes the raw matrix. The shrinkage factor collapses and the estimator
degenerates into the global mean --- and it does so \emph{monotonically in
$K$}, so it fails harder at the batch sizes GRPO is actually deployed at.
\textbf{(ii)} Estimating $V$ from the data --- equivalently, applying the
specification after the reward rescaling it assumes --- lowers the baseline's
MSE by 16--54\% in simulation. The gradient-noise reduction an earlier draft
of this report claimed ($-21.4\%$ $[-51.5,-7.8]$) came from a projection with
a bug and from rollouts drawn with a repetition penalty the policy does not
have (Sec.~\ref{sec:corrections}); it is withdrawn pending remeasurement.
\textbf{(iii)} The
specification's optional ``divide by the within-group standard deviation'' is
unsafe in combination with shrinkage, inflating advantages by $10^4$.

I also report a measurement I think matters more than any of the above. With
the corrected sampler, three seeds and both group shapes at $K\cdot N=64$, no
baseline differs from GRPO in final accuracy by more than its own seed-to-seed
spread; the one row that reaches $p<0.01$ (\texttt{js\_fixed} at $N=2$,
$-3.0\pm0.3$) is algebraically the \emph{same algorithm} as the
\texttt{global} arm that sits at $-0.2\pm1.6$, and the two arms' three
same-seed runs end $0.5$, $6.5$ and $1.5$ points apart. That is the
run-to-run term; the effect under test is $0.65$--$1.5$ points. This is why
the headline measurement here is gradient noise on paired rollouts rather
than end-to-end accuracy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Task}

Given \texttt{GRPO.md}: replace GRPO's per-group mean baseline with a
James--Stein shrinkage estimator, on GSM8K, with a small policy model. The
specification places the change precisely --- ``after the reward matrix is
formed and before advantages are computed'' --- so what is being rewritten is
the \emph{baseline}, not the reward function.

\paragraph{Setup.} Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct (full fine-tune, no LoRA), GSM8K
(7473 train / 1319 test), rule-based verifiable reward (no reward model), one
RTX~5080 (16\,GB, sm\_120). $K=8$ prompts $\times\,N=8$ generations, 512 new
tokens, bf16 with gradient checkpointing; 11.0\,GiB peak, ${\sim}25$\,s/step.

\section{Corrections (2026-09-04)}
\label{sec:corrections}

Four defects were found in review after the first draft. All are fixed in
the code; none of the numbers below has been re-measured since.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Off-policy rollouts.} \texttt{generate()} set temperature,
      top-$p$ and top-$k$ but not \texttt{repetition\_penalty}, which fell
      through to the checkpoint's \texttt{generation\_config} --- 1.1 for
      Qwen2.5-Instruct. Completions were sampled from a penalised
      distribution while the loss used the raw policy's log-probabilities:
      off-policy without an importance correction, in every arm of every
      experiment (training sweep, gradient measurement, evaluation, prompt
      selection). The loop now passes 1.0 explicitly. Between-arm
      comparisons remain paired and internally consistent, but they compare
      baselines under a sampler that is not $\pi_\theta$.
\item \textbf{Projection bug in the gradient measurement.} The Hutchinson
      probes reused random entries across equal-shaped parameter tensors, so
      the covariance estimate was invalid. Fixed (continuous stream per
      probe); Sec.~\ref{sec:gradvar} is therefore historical.
\item \textbf{Completion mask.} Only \texttt{<|im\_end|>} counted as a stop
      token; Qwen2.5 also stops on \texttt{<|endoftext|>}, which is its pad
      token, so such a completion was scored as 512 valid tokens including
      padding. The mask now stops on every terminator plus the pad id.
\item \textbf{Shrinkage diagnostic.} Groups whose mean equals the grand mean
      were logged as ``unshrunk'' (1.0) rather than undefined; this is why
      \texttt{global} shows $0.025$ in Table~\ref{tab:sweep} instead of its
      definitional $0$.
\end{enumerate}

Beyond the bugs, the noise/signal ratio itself was fragile: $\lVert\E[g]\rVert^2$
was obtained as the exact $\E\lVert g\rVert^2$ minus the probe estimate of
$\trcov(g)$, and it is only 8\% of $\E\lVert g\rVert^2$ here, so a few percent
of probe error --- which more batches do not reduce --- dominates the ratio.
The re-measurement will use, alongside the probes, an exact probe-free
estimator: for independent batches $\E\langle g_b,g_{b'}\rangle=\lVert\E[g]\rVert^2$,
so disjoint consecutive pairs of batches give unbiased samples of both
$\lVert\E[g]\rVert^2$ and $\E\lVert g\rVert^2$ (the paper's own instrument,
its eq.~17--18, is the same idea across micro-batches). The training loop now
also logs that per-step estimator on request, so the specification's
stability claim can be compared across arms on the same batches; the
small-$N$ runs the specification motivates are planned with it.

\section{Method}

\subsection{What the specification says}

With group means $X_k=\frac1N\sum_i r_i^{(k)}$ and grand mean $\bar X$,
\begin{equation}
\tilde\mu_k=\bar X+\shrink\cdot(X_k-\bar X),
\qquad
\shrink=\max\!\left(0,\;1-\frac{c}{S}\right),
\label{eq:js}
\end{equation}
where $S=\sum_k (X_k-\bar X)^2$ and $c=V(K-3)$ with $V=\Var(X_k)$ assumed
equal to $1/N$. Advantages are $A_i^{(k)}=r_i^{(k)}-\tilde\mu_k$.

\subsection{The assumption that fails}

$V=1/N$ presumes per-sample reward variance $\sigma^2\approx1$. On GSM8K the
reward is binary, so $\sigma_k^2=p_k(1-p_k)\le\tfrac14$ --- at least $4\times$
smaller, and heteroscedastic across prompts (zero for a group that is entirely
right or entirely wrong). Overstating $V$ overstates $c$, which drives
$\shrink$ to zero.

The failure is not incidental; it is asymptotic in $K$. Since
$\E[S]\approx(K-1)\Var(X_k)$ and $c=V(K-3)$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{c}{S}\;\xrightarrow[K\to\infty]{}\;
\frac{V_{\text{assumed}}}{\Var(X_k)}
=\frac{1/N}{\tau^2+\sigma^2/N},
\label{eq:limit}
\end{equation}
a constant. Whenever it exceeds 1, $\shrink\to0$ and the estimator becomes the
global mean. For the reward distribution used here
($V_{\text{assumed}}=0.125$, $\Var(X_k)=0.074$) the limit is $1.69>1$: the
collapse is certain, and small $K$ only escapes it by sampling noise in $S$.

\paragraph{Five latent defects in the specification.}
Each is handled in the code and asserted by a test; none changes the
estimator where the specification is well defined.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Step 7 is unreachable.} The prose says ``$S=0\Rightarrow$
      shrink factor $1$''; the listing crashes before reaching it, on exactly
      the case it claims to handle --- and that case occurs at $K=8$ with
      $0/1$ rewards. We clamp to $0$ instead, which is observationally
      identical: $S=0$ forces $\mathrm{diff}=0$, so $\tilde\mu_k=\bar X$ for
      any factor.
\item \textbf{Step 5 contradicts the listing.} The prose returns the
      per-group means for $K<4$; the listing, once its indexing is repaired,
      returns rewards centred on the \emph{global} mean --- the
      \texttt{global} arm, which is no longer GRPO. We follow the prose.
\item \textbf{The Sec.~2 precondition is missing from the listing.} The theory
      assumes rewards rescaled so that $\sigma^2\approx1$, which is where
      $V=1/N$ comes from; the listing takes the raw matrix. With $0/1$
      rewards $\sigma^2\le\tfrac14$, so $c$ is at least $4\times$ too large:
      the shrink factor averages $0.2$ at $K=8$ and meets the global mean by
      $K=32$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:collapse}). \texttt{js\_pooled} is the listing
      with the rescaling restored, using the pooled within-group standard
      deviation because the per-group one is $0$ for 30--40\% of groups.
\item \textbf{The optional ``divide by the within-group std'' is unsafe
      under shrinkage.} A degenerate group has std $0$ and, by design, a
      non-zero shrunk advantage, so $A/(0+10^{-4})$ scales it by $10^4$
      (Sec.~\ref{sec:footgun}).
\item \textbf{Stein's guarantee does not transfer as stated.} It needs a
      common, known $V$ and $K\ge4$; Bernoulli rewards have
      $V_k=p_k(1-p_k)/N$, different for every prompt and overstated
      $4\times$ by $V=1/N$. And a lower-MSE baseline is not an unbiased
      gradient: the shrunk $\tilde\mu_k$ depends on $r_i^{(k)}$ itself, so
      only the leave-one-out variants (\texttt{rloo}, \texttt{js\_loo}) keep
      the policy gradient unbiased. The specification's ``more stable, faster
      convergence, smaller $N$'' comes with no measurement; the variance
      diagnostic and the small-$N$ runs of Sec.~\ref{sec:corrections} exist
      to supply one.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Result 2 (withdrawn): the historical gradient-noise measurement}
\label{sec:gradvar}

\textbf{Every number in this section was produced by the buggy projection
and the 1.1 sampler of Sec.~\ref{sec:corrections}. It is kept as a record of
the instrument, not as a result.}

Comparing training runs is the wrong instrument for this (Sec.~\ref{sec:floor}).
Instead every baseline scores the \emph{same} rollouts, so rollout randomness
cancels and the baseline is the only thing that differs; the policy is frozen,
so this measures the estimator rather than a training outcome. The reported
quantity is $\trcov(g)/\lVert\E[g]\rVert^2$ --- scale-invariant, because the
baselines produce gradients whose magnitudes differ ${\sim}5\times$ and raw
$\trcov$ would merely rank them by scale.

\midrule
\texttt{vanilla}    & 11.5 & ---        & ---              & \\
\texttt{rloo}       & 11.5 & $-0.3\%$   & $[-2.5,+6.9]$    & no difference (instrument check) \\
\texttt{js\_loo}    & \textbf{9.1} & $\mathbf{-21.4\%}$ & $\mathbf{[-51.5,-7.8]}$ & significant \\
\texttt{js\_pooled} & 9.9  & $-14.4\%$  & $[-38.2,+0.8]$   & marginal \\
\texttt{js\_fixed}  & 6.0  & $-48.1\%$  & $[-81.3,-15.8]$  & biased --- see below \\

\paragraph{\texttt{js\_fixed} and \texttt{global} look best here, and that
reading is wrong.} Only leave-one-out baselines are independent of the reward
they are subtracted from, so only \texttt{rloo} and \texttt{js\_loo} are
unbiased for the same gradient. The other two target a \emph{different}
$\E[g]$: handing an all-correct group a positive advantage inflates
$\lVert\E[g]\rVert^2$ with a component that merely pushes up easy prompts. A
larger denominator is not more signal.

That \texttt{rloo}$\,\approx\,$\texttt{vanilla} to within $[-2.5,+6.9]$ is the
check that the instrument resolves anything at all --- and it agrees with the
synthetic study, where leave-one-out changes the baseline's independence, not
its accuracy.

\section{Result 3: the standard-deviation variant is a footgun}
\label{sec:footgun}

The specification offers ``divide by the within-group standard deviation
afterwards'' as an option. Under plain GRPO that is safe on a degenerate group
only because the advantage is exactly zero there, so $0/(0+10^{-4})=0$. Every
shrinkage baseline gives such a group a \emph{non-zero} advantage by design ---
that is the point of the method --- so the same expression multiplies it by
$10^4$. Measured on GSM8K at $K=8,N=8$ with 38\% degenerate groups:
$\Var(A)=1.07\times10^5$, $\lVert g\rVert=1.7\times10^3$. Leaving zero-spread
groups unscaled gives $0.57$ and $1.2$.

positive-part clamp fires on every batch, so \texttt{js\_fixed} computes
exactly the \texttt{global} baseline: its logged shrinkage is $0$ on all
150 steps of all three seeds, and its step-1 record (reward, gradient norm,
completion length) agrees with \texttt{global}'s to the bit before the two
processes diverge at step~2.

single-seed accuracy comparison here, in either direction, is an artefact.

\paragraph{At $K=8$, $N=8$ nothing is resolved.} Every arm is within 1.2
points of \texttt{vanilla}, every sem exceeds the difference, every $p$ is
about $0.6$. The per-seed columns show why: the same arm moves 3--5 points
between seeds. Seed~0 alone would have said \texttt{global} beats
\texttt{vanilla} by 4.0 points (McNemar $p=0.039$, 10 wins / 2 losses);
seeds 1 and 2 put it at $+2.0$ and $-2.5$.

\paragraph{At $K=32$, $N=2$ the one significant row is the noise floor,
measured.} \texttt{js\_fixed} at $-3.0\pm0.3$ ($p=0.009$) reads as a
finding, but Sec.~\ref{sec:floor} shows it is algebraically \texttt{global}
at this shape, and \texttt{global} sits at $-0.2\pm1.6$. Pooling the two
arms as the six runs of one algorithm they are gives $-1.6\pm1.0$ against
\texttt{vanilla}, and the significance is gone. A $t$-test with two degrees
of freedom on three differences that happen to land within $0.5$ of each
other produces $p<0.01$ from nothing; the row is kept in the table to show
the trap.

\paragraph{What the sweep does establish.} Two things. The specification
verbatim, at the $N=2$ it is motivated by, does not merely over-shrink: it discards
the group structure on 100\% of steps (25--30\% at $N=8$), which is
Result~1 observed in training rather than in simulation.
\texttt{js\_pooled}, the specification with its $\sigma^2\approx1$
precondition honoured, keeps 56\% of the structure at $N=2$ and 85\% at
$N=8$ --- the mild shrinkage Stein's result asks for. And the ``more stable
training'' claim finds no support at this scale: no arm clipped a single
gradient step under the 1.0 sampler (the clip counts of
Table~\ref{tab:sweep} were an off-policy artefact), sampled-token entropy
falls from $0.52$ to $0.26$--$0.33$ for every arm alike, and the per-step
gradient noise/signal ratio of \cite{zeng2025shrinking} (eq.~17--18,
logged with \texttt{--track-grad-var}) is 58--182 with 32--45\% of steps
returning a signal estimate $\le0$: at 8 or 32 prompts per step the
gradient is essentially all noise for every baseline, and the ratio cannot
separate them. The comparison script reports those steps as right-censored
($+\infty$) with their share, rather than dropping them, which would bias
the median down.

\section{Training runs, seed 0, 1.1 sampler (superseded)}

The first sweep, kept for the record; its stability differences (clip
counts, entropy) are off-policy artefacts that vanish in
Sec.~\ref{sec:phase4b}. Five arms, 150 steps each, identical seed so every arm walks the same prompt
stream (verified: identical reward and degenerate-group fractions at step~0).
Every arm evaluates the same 200 test problems in the same order, so
checkpoints are compared with McNemar rather than by overlapping binomial
intervals.

\paragraph{Nothing here is significant, in either direction.} No arm improves
on its own starting point at $p<0.05$, and no arm differs from
\texttt{vanilla}: against \texttt{vanilla}'s final checkpoint,
\texttt{global} is $+2.0$ ($p=0.54$), \texttt{js\_fixed} $+1.5$ ($p=0.68$),
\texttt{js\_loo} $+0.0$ ($p=1.00$), \texttt{js\_pooled} $-0.5$ ($p=1.00$).
This is exactly what Sec.~\ref{sec:floor} predicts: the spread across all five
arms is 2.5 points, below the 3.0-point floor a single arm shows against
itself. \textbf{The sweep does not show that shrinkage helps training, and it
was not capable of showing it.}

It is worth naming the trap in Table~\ref{tab:sweep}: \texttt{global} has the
largest nominal gain. Read without the $p$ column it says ``throw away the
group structure entirely and GRPO improves'', which is both unsupported and
contrary to Sec.~\ref{sec:floor}.

\paragraph{What the sweep does establish is structural.} The shrinkage column
separates cleanly into three tiers that accuracy cannot resolve but the
estimator's own behaviour makes unambiguous:

\begin{center}\small
\texttt{vanilla} $1.000$ \quad$\gg$\quad
\texttt{js\_pooled} $0.850$, \texttt{js\_loo} $0.818$ \quad$\gg$\quad
\texttt{js\_fixed} $0.202$ \quad$\gg$\quad \texttt{global} $0.025$
\end{center}

The two corrected variants retain ${\sim}82$--$85\%$ of the group structure ---
mild shrinkage, which is what Stein's result asks for --- while the specified
estimator retains $20\%$ and discards it outright on $43\%$ of steps. That
\texttt{js\_pooled} and \texttt{js\_loo}, derived independently, land within
$0.03$ of each other is mutual corroboration; it also raises confidence in the
$(n-1)/n$ factor I could not verify against the paper.

At $K=8$, \texttt{js\_fixed} is degraded but not identical to \texttt{global}
($0.202$ vs $0.025$). Full coincidence needs larger $K$, as Table~2 shows ---
which is the deployment regime, not this one.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{No end-to-end accuracy claim.} Three seeds per arm at two
      group shapes leave every difference inside its own sem
      (Table~\ref{tab:phase4b}), and the same-algorithm pair of
      Table~\ref{tab:twins} spans $0.5$--$6.5$ points. Resolving a
      1-point effect at this run-to-run spread needs on the order of
      20--40 seeds per arm.
\item \textbf{48 batches was not enough} for the gradient measurement --- every
      interval crossed zero. The reported result needed 200. Effect sizes should
      not be read off short runs here.
\item \textbf{\texttt{js\_loo} is verified} against the arXiv PDF (Sec.~3.3,
      eq.~10 and 13--16; the $(n-1)/n$ factor is eq.~15).
\item \textbf{The specification's regime is tested for accuracy and
      stability, not for gradient noise.} At $N=2$ the per-step
      noise/signal estimator returns a non-positive signal on 36--46\% of
      steps for every arm; the paired-rollout measurement of Result~2
      (200 batches, both estimators) has not yet been rerun with the 1.0
      sampler.
\item \textbf{Single model, single task.} Whether the collapse threshold moves
      with reward density is untested.
\end{itemize}
